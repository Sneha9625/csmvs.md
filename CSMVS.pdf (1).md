# **1\. Brief History**

CSMVS traces its origin to 14 August 1904, when a group of prominent citizens of Bombay, including Sir Pherozeshah Mehta, Justice Badruddin Tyabji, Narotamdas Gokuldas, Justice Chandavarkar and Sassoon J. David met at the Town Hall and resolved to build a public museum as a memorial to the visit of the Prince of Wales (later King George V). The foundation stone was laid by the Prince of Wales on 11 November 1905, and the institution was named the Prince of Wales Museum of Western India. It was built on the Government-granted 'Crescent Site' in south Mumbai and funded through public donations supported by the Government of the Bombay Presidency.

The building was designed by Scottish architect George Wittet, selected through an open competition in 1909, in the Indo-Saracenic style \[ a blend of Hindu, Islamic (Saracenic) and Western architectural elements\]. Construction was completed in 1914, but the building was first used as a military hospital (the Lady Hardinge War Hospital) during the First World War and later during the 1918–1920 influenza pandemic, before finally opening to the public on 10 January 1922.

The Museum was renamed Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya in 2001, in honour of the Maratha ruler Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj. It was declared a Grade I Heritage Building in 1990 and, in 2018, became part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site 'Victorian Gothic and Art Deco Ensembles of Mumbai'. The Museum completed 100 years on 10 January 2022 and today houses an encyclopaedic collection of roughly 70,000 artefacts spanning sculpture, terracotta, bronzes, Indus Valley excavated material, Indian miniature paintings, decorative arts, numismatics, natural history specimens and more.

## **Key Milestones**

- 1904–05: Public resolution and foundation-stone laying; museum named Prince of Wales Museum of Western India.
- 1909: Board of Trustees constituted by Act; George Wittet selected as architect.
- 1914: Main building completed; used as a war hospital through WWI and the 1918–20 pandemic.
- 10 January 1922: Formally opened to the public.
- 1938: Extension Wing opened.
- 1990: Declared a Grade I Heritage Building.
- 2001: Renamed Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (CSMVS).
- 2010: Received the UNESCO Asia-Pacific Award for Cultural Heritage Conservation.
- 2015: Museum on Wheels outreach programme launched.
- 2018: Declared part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- 2019: City's first Children's Museum opened; Museum received IGBC Platinum green-building rating.
- 2022: Museum completed 100 years; received a second UNESCO Asia-Pacific Award for Cultural Heritage Conservation.

# **2\. Vision / Mission**

CSMVS's own published aim (csmvs.in) states:

_"Our aim at Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Vastu Sangrahalaya (CSMVS) is to create awareness and sensitivity towards our rich heritage through a visitor-friendly museum for the purposes of education, study and enjoyment of the public."_

This purpose echoes the institution's founding intent, articulated in 1922 by the then Chairman of the Board of Trustees, J. T. Brander: the Museum was meant to be "largely an institution or foundation for promoting research in all branches of science and knowledge and not only a museum of curiosities for the sightseer." In its Centenary communications, CSMVS also describes itself as "a museum for the people, by the people" and as a cultural and educational space that touches all aspects of human life, going beyond being a mere storehouse of art and antiquities.

# **3\. Top Leadership**

Under his leadership, CSMVS has carried out extensive modernisation - refurbishment of the heritage building, establishment of a conservation centre, a new Children's Museum, an institutional archive, new galleries and educational programmes. He received the UNESCO Asia-Pacific Heritage Award recognition (2010) for the Museum and an honorary doctorate from the University of Edinburgh.
2. **Ajay Kochle:** Assistant Director – Administration. Over two decades with CSMVS, handling finance, general administration and project compliance.

# **4\. Key / Major Activities**

- **International Collaborations:** Hosts large-scale cultural events in partnerships with global titans like the British Museum, the Getty Foundation, and the Staatliche Museen zu Berlin.
- **Thematic Galleries:** Actively operates specialized centers like the _Mumbai Gallery_ (showcasing city-centric communities) and prehistoric research spaces.
- **Museum on Wheels:** A flagship mobile educational initiative consisting of specialized custom buses that take physical artifacts and digital exhibitions directly to rural and urban schools across Maharashtra and Gujarat.
- **Children's Museum:** Mumbai's first dedicated creative cultural lab designed specifically for children to conceptualize, manage, and engage with customized educational displays.
- **Digital Archiving and Conservation:** Conducts institutional archiving using state-of-the-art scanners to transform historical documents, delicate textiles, and letters into long-term digital formats.
- **Academic Programs:** Offers a formal _Postgraduate Diploma Programme in Museology and Art Conservation_ affiliated directly with the University of Mumbai.
- **Research Publications:** Publishes an annual peer-reviewed _Research Journal_ promoting original work in art history and archaeology.
- **Digital and Public Engagement:** It maintains online exhibition resources, digital content, social media channels and online ticketing facilities.

The museum also publishes research, newsletters, exhibition material and educational resources.

# **6\. Global Accolades & "Firsts"**

**1) The UNESCO Pinnacle (2022):** In its centenary year, CSMVS won the highly prestigious **UNESCO Asia-Pacific Award of Excellence for Cultural Heritage Conservation**. This is the highest honour given for heritage restoration in the region. The jury praised the museum team for executing world-class restorations of its massive dome and structure _during_ the heights of the COVID-19 pandemic lockdowns.

**2) First Institutional Archive in India:** CSMVS is the first museum in India to set up a dedicated, state-of-the-art **Institutional Archive Room**. It documents everything from hand-written letters from 1905 to old blueprints of the building's construction.

**3) First Green Museum in Asia:** Long before environmental sustainability became a standard corporate mandate, CSMVS became a pioneer. In 2019, it secured the **Platinum Green Building Certification** from the Indian Green Building Council (IGBC) - the highest possible rating. It runs a massive 70 kWp rooftop solar PV system, practices active rainwater harvesting, and runs on a "zero waste-to-landfill" target.

**4)** **It Was a Hospital First:** Although the iconic building was structurally completed in 1914, it didn't open as a museum right away. Because of World War I, the British military requisitioned the entire building to use as a **Military Hospital and Children's Welfare Exhibition Centre** before handing it back to the trust in 1920.

# **7\. Conclusions**

## CSMVS is more than a traditional museum housing historical objects. It functions as a centre for **heritage preservation, education, research, conservation, exhibitions and public engagement**

Its initiatives such as **Museum on Wheels, the Children's Museum, educational workshops, conservation programmes and international exhibitions** demonstrate its effort to make heritage accessible to diverse audiences.

Through its combination of historical collections, modern museum practices, digital initiatives and international collaborations, CSMVS continues to play an important role in connecting India's cultural heritage with contemporary audiences and global cultural institutions.

**_Work Activities Carried Out in Museums_**

**_A Brief Overview, Illustrated with Reference to CSMVS, Mumbai_**

**_Internship Activity A2_**

**_Modern museums run far more activity than the galleries a visitor sees. Below is a brief description of_**

**_the major work streams typically carried out inside a large museum, with real examples from CSMVS,_**

**_Mumbai's practice (verified against csmvs.in) noted under each heading._**

**_1\. On-Premise Galleries_**

**_Permanent and semi-permanent thematic galleries physically housed in the museum building, organised_**

**_by subject, period or medium (e.g., sculpture, decorative arts, natural history, coins). CSMVS's premises_**

**_house galleries covering Indus Valley archaeology, Indian sculpture and bronzes, miniature paintings,_**

**_Chinese and Japanese decorative art, European painting, arms and armour, natural history and_**

**_numismatics, among others, drawn from its collection of roughly 70,000 artefacts._**

**_2\. Online Galleries_**

**_Digital versions of galleries or curated object sets made accessible over the internet, through a_**

**_museum's own website, dedicated microsites, or third-party platforms such as Google Arts & Culture._**

**_These extend access to audiences who cannot visit in person and often include virtual tours, high-_**

**_resolution object images and interpretive text. CSMVS maintains an online presence through csmvs.in,_**

**_dedicated exhibition microsites (e.g., the Networks of the Past study-gallery site), and a partnership with_**

**_Google Arts & Culture._**

**_3\. Exhibitions_**

**_Time-bound, thematically curated displays, either drawn from the museum's own collection or_**

**_organised with loans from partner institutions designed around a specific narrative or research theme._**

**_These require curatorial research, object selection, loan agreements, design and marketing. CSMVS has_**

**_run major international collaborative exhibitions such as India and the World – A History in Nine Stories_**

**_(2017, with the British Museum) and Ancient Sculptures: India Egypt Assyria Greece Rome (2023–24,_**

**_with the British Museum, J. Paul Getty Museum and Staatliche Museen zu Berlin), alongside its_**

**_Networks of the Past study gallery._**

**_4\. Conservation & Preservation_**

**_Scientific and technical work to stabilise, clean, repair and protect artefacts and the museum building_**

**_itself, carried out by trained conservators using preventive and remedial techniques. This also covers_**

**_building conservation - structural repair, façade restoration and heritage-compliant maintenance._**

**_CSMVS operates a dedicated Art Conservation Centre and has run a multi-year comprehensive_**

**_conservation programme for its Grade I heritage building (from 2019), earning it two UNESCO Asia-_**

**_Pacific Awards for Cultural Heritage Conservation (2010 and 2022)._**

**_5\. Digitisation of Museum Artefacts_**

**_Creating high-resolution photographic and metadata records of objects, archives and documents for_**

**_research, conservation reference, publication and online display. This typically uses specialised_**

**_collection-management software. CSMVS's Archives and Documentation Department has digitised_**

**_thousands of exhibits, archival photographs and coins as part of a CSMVS–TCS Foundation project_**

**_(2019–2022), using the JATAN collection-management software developed by C-DAC._**

**_6\. Physical and Digital Collections Management_**

**_The systematic recording, cataloguing, storage, condition-tracking and movement-control of every_**

**_object a museum holds - physically (storerooms, display cases, environmental control) and digitally_**

**_(collection databases, accession registers, object metadata). This underpins conservation, research,_**

**_loans and exhibition planning. CSMVS's Postgraduate Diploma in Museology & Conservation curriculum_**

**_reflects this, covering collection policy, documentation systems and relevant legislation (e.g., the_**

**_Antiquities and Art Treasures Act)._**

**_7\. Educational Activities_**

**_Programmes that translate the collection into learning experiences for schools, colleges, families and the_**

**_general public - guided tours, workshops, lecture series, teacher training and academic courses. CSMVS_**

**_runs a full-year Postgraduate Diploma in Museology & Conservation (with the University of Mumbai),_**

**_maintains a dedicated Children's Museum (India's first, opened 2019), and operates the Citi-CSMVS_**

**_Museum on Wheels, two custom-built buses that carry travelling exhibitions and workshops to schools_**

**_across Maharashtra, Gujarat and neighbouring states._**

**_8\. Conferences & Seminars_**

**_Academic and professional gatherings - lectures, seminars, symposia and training workshops, that bring_**

**_together museum staff, scholars and conservation professionals to share research and practice. CSMVS_**

**_regularly hosts and co-hosts lectures and training sessions with international partners (for example,_**

**_technical art history and conservation-science sessions run with the Harvard Art Museums' Straus_**

**_Center)._**

**_9\. Ticketing_**

**_Managing visitor entry pricing (including concessions for students, children and international visitors),_**

**_counter and/or online ticket sales, special-exhibition surcharges, and photography permits. Ticketing_**

**_revenue is also a funding stream for museum operations. CSMVS sells general admission and separate_**

**_special-exhibition tickets, with an additional charge for photography, and typically publishes current_**

**_rates and hours on its official website._**

**_10\. On-Premise Library_**

**_A reference and research library, generally serving curators, conservators, students and researchers,_**

**_holding books, journals, exhibition catalogues, archival material and institutional records. CSMVS_**

**_maintains a library at its Mumbai premises supporting its research, curatorial and postgraduate-diploma_**

**_activities._**

**_11\. On-Premise and Online Museum Shops_**

**_Retail outlets physical and, increasingly, web-based: selling books, replicas, souvenirs and merchandise_**

**_related to the collection, which also serve as a revenue and outreach channel. CSMVS operates an on-_**

**_site museum shop; many national museums, including CSMVS, are also expanding merchandise access_**

**_through websites and e-commerce partners._**

**_12\. Fundraising_**

**_Raising funds through memberships, corporate sponsorships, government/CSR grants, philanthropic_**

**_donations and adoption schemes, to support conservation, digitisation, exhibitions and education._**

**_CSMVS runs an 'Adopt a Treasure/Masterpiece/Antiquity' scheme inviting citizens and organisations to_**

**_fund the care of specific objects, and has partnered with sponsors such as the TCS Foundation (building_**

**_conservation and digitisation), Citibank (Museum on Wheels) and the Rotary Club of Bombay (building_**

**_illumination project)._**

**_13\. Interactions with Other Museums & Institutions_**

**_Inter-museum collaboration through object loans, co-curated exhibitions, staff exchange, shared_**

**_research, and joint training programmes, both domestic and international. CSMVS has active, long-_**

**_standing partnerships with institutions such as the British Museum (London), Victoria and Albert_**

**_Museum (London), J. Paul Getty Museum, Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, the Archaeological Survey of_**

**_India, the Directorate of Archaeology and Museums (Government of Maharashtra), and the Bihar_**

**_Museum, Patna, among others._**
